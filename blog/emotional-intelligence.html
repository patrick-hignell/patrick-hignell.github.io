Emotional Intelligence

What is Emotional Intelligence?

Emotional Intelligence (EI) is a measure of how perceptive a person is regarding how they and the people around them are feeling. People with high EI tend to have greater self satisfaction, work better in groups, and can be good leaders. The five components of EI are-

  • Self-awareness
    • Being aware of what you are feeling as you are feeling it. Being able to detect the feelings of others around you. Understanding your strengths and weaknesses. Being able to challenge yours or others assumptions.
  • Self-regulation
    • Being in control of your emotions without letting them control you or trying to suppress them. The ability to react calmly in stressful situations.
  • Motivation
    • Understanding not only what you want to do but why you want to do it. Understanding what motivates others.
  • Empathy
    • Understanding a situation from another's perspective. If they come to a different conclusion, understanding how they got to that conclusion.
  • Social skills
    • Social skills or “soft skills” involve being able to make an emotional connection with someone, knowing how to manage conflict, handle bad news and motivate others.

How is EQ different to IQ?

Intelligence Quotient (or IQ) is a measure of a person's intellectual capability. There are several different standardised tests, the aim of which is to compare the tester to the average distribution of test takers. The final score is standardised on a bell curve, with 100 being average at the peak of the curve, with outliers found at either end of the curve. 68% (~2/3) of people have an IQ of between 85 and 115. ~95% of people have an IQ of between 70 and 130. People with an IQ of <70 (two standard deviations below the average) are in the bottom 2% and may have an intellectual disability. People with IQ above 130 are in the top 2% and are often considered highly intelligent. The Mensa society accepts people with an IQ in the highest 98th percentile (typically 132 on a standardised test).

IQ tests tend to focus on 6 key areas-

  • Language
  • Reasoning abilities
  • Processing speed
  • Visual-spatial processing
  • Memory
  • Math

Whilst IQ is a measure of a person's capacity to retain and apply knowledge, EQ is a measure of a person's capacity to perceive and manage their own and others emotions and to communicate effectively. High levels of both are required for effective problem solving, teamwork and leadership.

Why is EQ Important?

Meta-analysis of studies involving testing EQ have found that individuals with higher EQ generally have better job satisfaction, better job performance, lower job stress, higher job commitment, build better intra-organisational relationship and show higher altruism than individuals with lower EQ. Employers often look for evidence of high EQ during the job application process.
