Human Skills

What are three new things you have learnt about yourself and your ego due to the Human Skills learning?

I learnt that a lot of the skills I have learned in psychiatry are applicable to Human Skills, however I also learnt that what I have less experience with is being more vulnerable about my own shortcomings and difficulties with my colleagues. I am used to being more vulnerable with a supervisor as I am used to the student-supervisor relationship, however I was noticing whilst looping with colleagues that whilst I found the part of actively listening to others familiar, being vulnerable whilst being actively listened to was more disconcerting as I am less used to this practice in my previous roles. I have continued to try and learn ways to keep my learning exciting by trying to relate each new lesson in a way which is of interest to me.

What are the role of values, empathy, and self-awareness in learning and programming?

Knowing your values helps you navigate difficult situations within a team by knowing when to speak up if there is a problem and knowing what type of culture you wish to foster with your teammates. Empathy can be useful to understand an issue from another person's perspective and help you resolve conflict. Self-awareness helps you identify the feelings that you are feeling so that you can factor these in when decision making, rather than invertedly allowing your emotions to affect decision making without your knowledge.

What has surprised you the most about the Human Skills learning?

I was surprised by the level of research behind the growth mindset and was excited by thinking about the opportunities for increasing equity in the education system.

What were the most challenging aspects of the Human Skills learning?

Trying to change my current way of working to incorporate all of the different aspects that we have learned about. Remembering to do the plans I have set for myself to try and maintain wellness, such as regularly engaging in mindfulness and the Te Whare Tapa Whā wellbeing plans.

Why do you think we, a programming school, are spending so much time focusing on Human Skills learning in a web development Bootcamp course?

Due to the importance of Human Skills when working in working in a team and hence the importance placed on them by prospective employers when hiring.

Does the time you spent studying Human Skills learning here feel like a waste of time? Should you have just used that time to practise programming instead? Justify your answer.

It is certainly not a waste of time! Human Skills learning sets us up for working within a team effectively which will be a large part of any job we go on to do! I think there is less of an immediate reward to learning Human Skills when compared to programming as I think the benefits of Human Skills training will only come to fruition once we are working in a team. Whenever I started to feel some discontent with the Human Skills training, I just had to think of it like eating my vegetables, I may not experience much in the way of an immediate reward, but it will be good for me in the long run!
